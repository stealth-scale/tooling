# Colours

Every colour a component may use is a token, and every theme defines all of them in both
modes. A component never writes a colour: it writes `bg-card` or `text-muted-foreground`, and
the theme decides what that is.

Everything below reads the theme and the mode the toolbar is on, so switching either changes
it. Where two colours are meant to be read together, the page prints the ratio they measure
against each other and the level it reaches: AAA is 7:1, AA is 4.5:1. Every theme holds text
on a surface to AAA. A fill clears the level the theme's recipe asked for, and the surfaces,
inks and edges do not move with it.

## Three ways a colour is used

A token is one of three things, and a component uses it as that thing:

- A **pair** is a fill and the text meant to sit on it: `bg-primary text-primary-foreground`.
  A component never takes a fill from one pair and a label from another.
- An **ink** is a tone as text on the page: `text-destructive-ink` for a soft badge, an
  alert's words, or a number that went the wrong way. A fill is solved to carry its own label,
  and at AA it is too light to be read as text, so a component never writes a fill as text.
- An **edge** separates, outlines or marks focus: `border-border`, `border-input`,
  `ring-ring`.

## Surfaces

The planes a screen is built from, back to front: the page, a card raised off it, a popover
raised off that. In light the three are near-white and a shadow does the separating; in dark
they climb a ladder and a hairline does.

## Emphasis

Four levels, from the one action a screen asks for down to text that is merely present.

## Status

Outcomes. A theme keeps these hues unless its recipe names its own, because a warning that
changes colour with the brand is a warning nobody learns to read. Each outcome carries a solid
fill for the thing that must be seen first, a soft one for the thing that sits inside something
else, and an ink for a number or a word on the page.

### Soft

The tinted surface a badge, a callout or a table cell takes where the solid fill would shout.
It is a lift off the page in the outcome's hue, so a theme that moves its paper carries these
with it, and the label on each is walked until it clears 7:1 against the surface it actually
sits on rather than against the page. That is the difference from tinting a fill by hand: a
component writing `destructive` at ten percent with the ink on top has a pair nothing measures.

## Outline

What separates a surface, what outlines a field, and what marks focus. `input` and `ring`
clear 3:1 against the surface they sit on, which is what a person needs to find a control.
`border` is decorative and stays a hairline. `ring` takes the primary's hue in every theme.

<Edges />

## Charts

Five series colours, in the order a chart assigns them, and three tones for a value that
carries a judgement. The series are not the emphasis colours: a series is data, not a call to
action. The tones are not the status colours either: a status fill is built to carry white
text, and as an arc in a chart it reads as brown.

## Code

## Effects

What a page draws over itself. `overlay` is the scrim under a dialog, and it is a token because
a black scrim at half opacity disappears on a dark page. `selection` is what the browser paints
behind selected text, and `highlight` is a search match. Both carry a foreground solved to
AAA, because selected text is still text.

## Gradient and glow

Three stops at one lightness, differing only in hue, from the primary to the accent. That is
what reads as a gradient; a band between two colours solved for contrast reads as a fade. The
glow is the colour a `shadow-glow-*` is thrown in.

<Swatches of="gradient" />

## Shadow ink

Every shadow mixes from `shadow`, with the opacity the mode allows. `shadow-highlight` is the
light along the top edge of a raised surface in dark mode, where a shadow is ink on ink and
what reads as an edge is light catching the top. In light it is transparent.

<Swatches of="shadow" />

## Adding one

A colour that is not on this page does not exist. Adding one means adding it to the contract,
which makes every theme fail to build until it has an answer. That failure is the point: it is
how a token stays defined everywhere.
